Trifid Cipher

Broken

Bifid extended to three dimensions — the 3×3×3 cube

Modern Lesson3D lookup tables in modern cipher design

Why This Matters

Delastelle’s Trifid uses three coordinates per symbol. Concatenating and regrouping those coordinates mixes information across a chosen period. The method is a useful comparison with Bifid, but three dimensions alone do not prove stronger security for every parameter choice.

Historical Context

Delastelle’s Trifid uses a 3×3×3 arrangement, giving 27 symbols, and combines three coordinate streams. His posthumous 1902 treatise describes the method. The extra symbol may be a period or another agreed symbol; it is not inherently a null that must be discarded.

How It Works
3×3×3 cube: each letter has coordinates (layer, row, col)

A=(1,1,1)  B=(1,1,2)  C=(1,1,3)
D=(1,2,1)  E=(1,2,2)  F=(1,2,3)
...

HELLO encrypted:
1. Get L,R,C coordinates for each letter
2. Write all L values, then R values, then C values
3. Group into triples, look up in cube
How It Was Broken
Statistical Cryptanalysis
Difficulty depends on period, key, text and attack

Three-coordinate fractionation changes how information is mixed across a block. Exactly which plaintext positions influence an output depends on the period and layout. A third coordinate alone does not prove that Trifid always requires more ciphertext to attack than Bifid.

What It Teaches Modern Cryptography
Concept from Trifid CipherModern Evolution
3D coordinate systemMulti-dimensional bit manipulations in modern hash functions
