Why This Matters

The coordinate idea in Polybius’s account of torch signalling has a later adaptation in the familiar letter grid. Such coordinates are used in ADFGX, Bifid, Nihilist, Trifid and tap-code constructions.

Historical Context

In Histories 10.45–47, Polybius describes a torch-signalling method devised by Cleoxenus and Democleitus and improved by him. The Greek alphabet was divided among five tablets. Two groups of torches identified the tablet and the letter within it; this was not a row signal followed after a pause by a column signal.

The grid became the foundation for numerous later ciphers — ADFGX, Bifid, Nihilist, and Trifid all build directly on Polybius's coordinate idea.

How It Works

Arrange the 25 letters (I and J share a cell) in a 5×5 grid. Each letter becomes a two-digit coordinate (row, column).

    1  2  3  4  5
1   A  B  C  D  E
2   F  G  H  IJ  K
3   L  M  N  O  P
4   Q  R  S  T  U
5   V  W  X  Y  Z

HELLO = 23 15 31 31 34

The modern 5×5 Latin grid shown here merges I/J. It is an adaptation of the coordinate idea, not the original Greek alphabet. An openly known grid is an encoding: no cryptanalysis is needed to reverse its pairs. A secretly permuted grid becomes a substitution cipher.

How It Was Broken
Frequency Analysis
Complexity: Trivial

For the public grid shown here, decoding is simply a lookup. If the alphabet is secretly permuted, each pair still corresponds to one letter and substitution analysis becomes relevant. Frequency rankings are hypotheses, especially for short texts; they are not a guaranteed recovery rule.
