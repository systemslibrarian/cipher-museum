Station HYPO — Pearl Harbor to Midway

From a basement under the Pearl Harbor submarine base, Joseph Rochefort's team changed the Pacific War in six months.

Broken (JN-25)

LocationPearl Harbor submarine base, Oahu, Hawaii
CommanderCommander Joseph Rochefort
PeriodFocus here: the 1941–1942 intelligence campaign
Key BreakJN-25, partially recovered — Imperial Japanese Navy main operational code
Critical InterceptMidway as "AF" — May 1942

Why This Matters

Station HYPO’s analysis helped Admiral Chester Nimitz anticipate the Japanese operation against Midway. Its work combined partial JN-25 recovery, traffic analysis and operational context. The false water-shortage message helped confirm an existing identification of the target designation AF. Intelligence gave US commanders an important opportunity; the battle’s outcome also depended on reconnaissance, command decisions and combat.

Before Pearl Harbor

Station HYPO worked alongside CAST in the Philippines and naval cryptanalysts in Washington. Before December 1941, JN-25 work did not provide readable operational orders revealing the Pearl Harbor attack. MAGIC diplomatic decrypts indicated worsening relations and the breakdown of negotiations; they were not a decrypted Japanese naval attack plan for Pearl Harbor. Diplomatic intelligence and naval operational intelligence must be distinguished.

The JN-25 Break

JN-25 combined a codebook of five-digit groups with a separate additive encipherment layer. Analysts sought reused additives, predictable message patterns and partial codebook meanings. Recovery was incremental and changed as new books and procedures appeared. Intelligence estimates depended on partial readings and traffic analysis, not on possession of a universally readable Japanese naval channel.

Midway: The Intelligence Win

By May 1942, HYPO’s team identified AF as Midway. A deliberately exposed water-shortage report and the resulting Japanese reference to AF supported that assessment. Nimitz positioned Enterprise, Hornet and Yorktown to meet the Japanese operation. Four Japanese fleet carriers were lost in the battle of 4–7 June, against the loss of Yorktown. The loss seriously weakened Japan’s experienced carrier force; it did not mean Japan could never build another carrier.

The Aftermath

Rochefort left command of HYPO later in 1942 amid disputes over intelligence assessments and credit. His contribution received formal posthumous recognition: the Reagan Presidential Library preserves the record of the Distinguished Service Medal presentation to his family on 30 May 1986. This recognition does not make any one unit the sole explanation for Midway’s outcome.
