Why This Matters

On 1 December 1948 an unidentified man was found dead on Somerton beach near Adelaide. In a fob pocket was the printed scrap 'Tamám Shud' ('it is ended'), torn from the last page of a copy of Edward FitzGerald's translation of Omar Khayyám's Rubáiyát. The book itself was later found, and inside its back cover, faintly pencilled, was a sequence of letters: MRGOABABD · MTBIMPANETP · MLIABOAIAQC · ITTMTSAMSTGAB. The lettering has no generally accepted interpretation; the simplified display above is not the complete marked page.

Historical Context

In July 2022 Colleen Fitzpatrick and Derek Abbott announced a genealogical identification as Carl “Charles” Webb using DNA from hair preserved in the death mask. That research announcement should be distinguished from an official police or coronial finding. ABC News reported on 6 July 2026 that police still described their coronial investigation as ongoing. This is a dated report, not a prediction about the eventual finding. Identification does not establish what the associated letters mean, or even that they form a cipher.

Technical Notes

The transcription is uncertain and the sample is short. Its frequency profile alone cannot establish its language, a one-time pad, a book cipher or an acrostic. A proposed interpretation needs independent evidence, not merely a phrase that can be made to fit.

Modern Echo

The case illustrates the distinction between identifying a person and interpreting an inscription. The 2022 researchers’ identification is a separate claim from any alleged solution to these letters.
