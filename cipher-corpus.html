Cipher Corpus

Known ciphertexts for learning, testing, and benchmarking classical cryptanalysis.

A curated library of ciphertext challenges with known solutions — 100,026 verified records across 82 cipher types, 84 cipher engines, and 9 languages. Each record includes the cipher type, key or settings, plaintext, difficulty, language, expected attack methods, and source information. Learners can practice codebreaking in Challenge Mode; tool builders can download the known-answer dataset for benchmarking cipher solvers.

Corpus Quality Rules

  • Every record must have verified plaintext.
  • Every record must identify the cipher type.
  • Every synthetic record must be labeled synthetic.
  • Every historical record must include source and rights information.
  • Every record must include a license.
  • Every record must include expected attack methods.
  • Every record must include text length.
  • Every record must state whether spacing and punctuation were preserved.
  • Every record must be reproducible where possible.
  • Known answers must be documented even if hidden in Challenge Mode.

For Learners and Educators

Use Cipher Corpus to practice identifying cipher types, testing attack methods, teaching frequency analysis, comparing difficulty levels, and understanding how text length, spacing, language, and key choice affect solvability.

  • Break a Caesar cipher by brute force
  • Compare long vs short substitution ciphertexts
  • Estimate Vigenère key length using index of coincidence
  • Use Kasiski examination on repeated patterns
  • Test Playfair digraph behavior
  • Compare transposition with substitution
  • Attempt a mixed cipher challenge
  • Measure how spacing removal changes difficulty

For Tool Builders

Cipher Corpus provides machine-readable known plaintext/ciphertext pairs for testing classical cipher solvers, cryptanalysis tools, and LLM-assisted cracking workflows.

import json
with open("all.jsonl", "r", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    for line in f:
        record = json.loads(line)
        print(record["id"], record["cipher_type"], record["ciphertext"])
Do not train and evaluate on the same split if you are measuring model performance. Future versions may include public and blind benchmark splits.

Known Limitations

Synthetic records (95,849 core + 4,122 multilingual/noisy extensions) are generated and roundtrip-verified using the Cipher Museum's 84 cipher engines. Historical records (55) are sourced from public-domain archives with full provenance. Synthetic records are useful for controlled testing but are not a substitute for historical cipher archives.
Classical ciphers vary widely in notation, alphabet handling, spacing, spelling, and operational use. Metadata is included to make those assumptions explicit.
Some codebook engines (Book Cipher, Arnold-André) produce fewer records due to reference-text letter coverage constraints — this is a property of the engine, not a corpus gap.
Multilingual records use ASCII transliteration of foreign language texts (diacritics removed) for A-Z engine compatibility — they test cipher mechanics with foreign language frequency patterns, not native character encodings.
Noisy variant records are not marked verified: true — noise injection means the exact roundtrip is not guaranteed.
This corpus is intended for education and benchmarking classical cryptanalysis, not for evaluating modern cryptographic security.

Why Classical Ciphers Break

Many classical ciphers leak patterns from the underlying language. Frequency analysis, repeated sequences, index of coincidence, n-gram scoring, and transposition patterns exploit this leakage.
Modern cryptography is designed so that ciphertext should not reveal useful statistical structure about the plaintext. Cipher Corpus helps learners see why that distinction matters.

Related Work & Attribution

Cipher Corpus builds on foundational work in LLM cryptanalysis evaluation, particularly CipherBank by Li et al. (2025), which established the first systematic benchmark for evaluating LLM cipher-breaking capabilities on classical ciphers.

CipherBank demonstrated that even advanced models like Claude-3.5 achieve only ~45% accuracy on classical cipher tasks, establishing the need for comprehensive evaluation infrastructure. Their pioneering work with 2,358 test cases across 9 cipher algorithms proved the viability of systematic cryptanalysis benchmarking.

Cipher Corpus extends this foundational approach with:

  • 100,026 test cases across 82 cipher algorithms (vs CipherBank's 2,358 across 9)
  • 55 historical records with verified provenance spanning 150 BCE–1999 CE
  • 9 languages (English, French, German, Latin, Spanish, Italian, Japanese, Russian, Chinese)
  • Noisy variants simulating OCR errors and telegraph noise
  • Live benchmark infrastructure with blind splits, LLM 3-shot export, and reproducibility receipts
  • Advanced cipher families including machine ciphers (Enigma, M-209, Lorenz), complex systems (ADFGX, VIC, Bazeries), and codebook ciphers

We gratefully acknowledge CipherBank's pioneering contribution to establishing this evaluation category.
