Chapter-to-Exhibit Reading Trails

Choose a book below. Each stop pairs a specific chapter or section with a museum activity and a question to investigate. These are concept companions, not reproductions of the books or complete implementations of their programming chapters. Use the stated edition: numbering can change between editions.

Sweigart · learn by doing Gaines · classical codebreaking Holden · the mathematics Esslinger · historical evidence

For a route without a book, use the museum's guided tours.

Cracking Codes with PythonAl Sweigart · No Starch Press, 2018

Read the author's free online book and chapter list. The museum illustrates cipher behavior; the book teaches the Python programs, file handling and testing.

  1. Chapters 5–6 · Caesar shifts and exhaustive search

    Try Caesar, then compare all shifts in Cipher Detective. What lets you recognize a promising plaintext?

    Read chapter 5 · Read chapter 6

  2. Chapters 7–12 · Transposition and testing

    Use Columnar Transposition to track moved letters. The museum's keyed-column variant differs from the book's width-key program; chapters 9–11 also cover software tests, files and English detection.

    Read chapter 7 · Read chapter 12

  3. Chapters 13–15 · Modular arithmetic and affine keys

    Try valid and invalid multipliers in Affine. Why must the multiplier have an inverse modulo 26?

    Read chapter 13 · Read chapter 15

  4. Chapters 16–17 · Substitution and word patterns

    Explore Monoalphabetic Substitution, then build a partial key in Cipher Detective. Which word candidates remain compatible with one shared mapping?

    Read chapter 16 · Read chapter 17

  5. Chapters 18–20 · Vigenère and repeated-key evidence

    Try Vigenère and the Kasiski tab in the Cryptanalysis Lab. Does a repeated fragment prove a key length, or suggest candidates?

    Read chapter 18 · Read chapter 20

  6. Chapter 21 · One-time pads

    Visit One-Time Pad. Separate the conditions for perfect secrecy from the effects of key reuse and ciphertext tampering.

    Read chapter 21

  7. Chapters 22–24 · Primes and public-key encryption

    Follow the small-number example in RSA. The arithmetic is educational: production RSA requires secure parameters, padding and implementation protections beyond this trail.

    Read chapter 22 · Read chapter 24

Cryptanalysis: A Study of Ciphers and Their SolutionHelen Fouché Gaines · corrected Dover edition, 1956; original work, 1939

A classical cryptanalysis workbook, not present-day deployment guidance. Find the publisher's edition (ISBN 978-0-486-20097-2); use your library copy for the chapter exercises.

  1. Chapters VI–VII · Columnar transposition and multiple anagramming

    Reorder several same-key intercepts in the multiple-anagramming exercise. Can one ordering explain every message? Compare the different-key control.

  2. Chapters VIII–IX · Substitution fundamentals

    Use Monoalphabetic Substitution and Cipher Detective to combine letter frequencies with word constraints. A frequent letter is evidence, not a guaranteed mapping.

  3. Chapters XII and XIV–XV · Vigenère and period hypotheses

    Move from Vigenère to the Cryptanalysis Lab. Compare Kasiski evidence with a guessed plaintext fragment; keep an inferred key period separate from verified text.

The Mathematics of SecretsJoshua Holden · Princeton University Press, 2017

Cryptography from Caesar Ciphers to Digital Encryption. See the author's book page, contents and errata. These stops use the 2017 edition (ISBN 978-0-691-14175-6).

  1. Sections 1.6–1.7 · Hill matrices and known plaintext

    Visit Hill. Check the determinant of the known-plaintext matrix: two matched blocks alone do not guarantee a unique 2 × 2 key.

  2. Sections 3.5–3.7 · Columnar transposition and anagramming

    Try the shared-column exercise. Compare plausible neighboring letters across messages; a contact score is not a probability of correctness.

  3. Sections 5.1–5.2 · Running keys and one-time pads

    Compare Running-Key and One-Time Pad. Why does a long passage without a short period still differ from independent, uniformly random key material?

Learning and Experiencing Cryptography with CrypTool and SageMathBernhard Esslinger · Artech House, 2024

The official companion page provides the contents, software and legal open-access chapters 1 and 3 (ISBN 978-1-68569-017-5). The museum is a separate implementation; it does not run the book's CrypTool or SageMath exercises.

  1. Chapter 2, sections 2.1–2.2 · Transposition and substitution

    Compare Columnar Transposition with Monoalphabetic Substitution. Which changes symbol positions, and which changes the symbols themselves?

  2. Chapter 3, sections 3.4.1–3.4.2 and 3.5.1 · Transcription and tokenization

    Prepare a source in Cipher Detective before analyzing it. Preserve uncertain symbols and original lineation, then inspect what an A–Z projection leaves out. For manuscript transcription at larger scale, explore CrypTool's CTTS project.

  3. Chapter 3, sections 3.5.2–3.5.3 · Heuristic search and scoring

    Compare hill climbing and simulated annealing in the Cryptanalysis Lab. A search score reflects a language model; it does not certify a historically correct reading.

Interactive Companion Exhibits

Standalone hands-on exhibits that go deeper on a single idea than the main halls can. Free, open-source, and browser-based.

Interactive Exhibit · Companion
DeckBook
A Cipher Museum companion exhibit

A card-based one-time keybook: the deck order is the key. Watch a Vigenère-style stream cipher run one card at a time, then break it yourself — reuse a key across two messages and crib-drag the plaintext back out — and finish with a shareable intercept challenge. A hands-on deep dive into one-time keys, key reuse, and why key distribution is the hard problem, all client-side and installable offline.

Canonical Histories

Six starting points for exploring cryptographic history and practice.

History · Essential
The Codebreakers
David Kahn
1967 · revised 1996 · ISBN 978-0684831305

A wide-ranging history of cryptology, from ancient writing systems to twentieth-century intelligence. The revised 1996 edition adds discussion of computer-era cryptography. Use it for historical context, with later specialist research for developments and corrections published since then.

Popular Science · Essential
The Code Book
Simon Singh
1999 · ISBN 978-0385495325

A narrative introduction to the history of cryptography, including Mary, Queen of Scots, Enigma, public-key cryptography and quantum ideas. It pairs well with the museum’s introductory exhibits; its historical narrative is not a current implementation guide.

Secret History: The Story of Cryptology
Craig P. Bauer
2nd ed. 2021 · ISBN 978-1138061231

A history-oriented textbook with mathematical explanations and cryptanalysis exercises. Use the second edition’s contents to locate the relevant system. Developments after its 2021 publication, including the 2023 Castelnau-letter decipherment, require separate sources.

Friedrich L. Bauer
4th ed. 2007 · ISBN 978-3540245025

A technical and historical reference on cryptography and cryptanalysis. Its fourth edition discusses classical cipher systems, mechanical methods and mathematical concepts such as redundancy and unicity distance. Read modern security recommendations alongside newer standards.

Modern Cryptography Textbooks

Textbooks and a narrative history connecting historical cryptography with modern theory and practice. Boneh and Shoup's textbook is freely available online.

Graduate Textbook
Introduction to Modern Cryptography
Jonathan Katz & Yehuda Lindell
Revised 3rd ed. 2025 · ISBN 978-1032496795

The standard graduate textbook for provable-security cryptography. Develops the modern definitional framework — semantic security, IND-CPA, IND-CCA, authenticated encryption — from first principles, with full proofs. Covers symmetric primitives, hash functions, public-key encryption, signatures, and key exchange. Used in cryptography courses worldwide. The book that defines what "modern" means in modern cryptography.

Publisher page → GraduateProvable Security
Free Online Textbook
A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography
Dan Boneh & Victor Shoup
Draft v0.6, 2023 · Free PDF

A free, ~900-page graduate textbook by two of the most influential cryptographers of the post-AES era. Covers the same provable-security ground as Katz–Lindell with deeper treatment of public-key topics (lattices, pairings, post-quantum), real-world protocols (TLS 1.3, Signal), and zero-knowledge proofs. The companion text to Boneh's Stanford Cryptography I Coursera course. Continually revised; cite the version.

Free PDF → FreeOpen Access
Foundational Textbook
Cryptography: Theory and Practice
Douglas R. Stinson & Maura B. Paterson
4th ed. 2018 · ISBN 978-1138197015

The classic mathematics-first cryptography textbook, now in a thoroughly revised fourth edition co-authored with Maura Paterson. Strongest book for the classical-to-modern transition: Shannon's information-theoretic security, perfect secrecy, the AES design rationale, and number-theoretic foundations of public-key cryptography are all covered with full mathematical detail and exercises.

Publisher page → TextbookMathematical
History · Public-Key Era
Crypto: How the Code Rebels Beat the Government
Steven Levy
2001 · ISBN 978-0140244328

The narrative history of the public-key revolution and the Crypto Wars of the 1990s — Diffie, Hellman, Merkle, Rivest–Shamir–Adleman, Zimmermann's PGP, the Clipper Chip, the export-control fight. Levy interviewed every major participant. The standard popular history of how civilian cryptography escaped the NSA's monopoly. Essential for anyone reading the museum's modern-era exhibits.

Publisher page → HistoryModern

Biography & Narrative History

Biography
Alan Turing: The Enigma
Andrew Hodges
1983 · centenary ed. 2014 · ISBN 978-0691164724

The standard biography of Turing, written by a mathematician who treats the technical material seriously. Covers Bletchley Park in depth, Turing's contributions to the Bombe design, his postwar computing and morphogenesis work, and his prosecution and death. The centenary edition includes a new preface and updated bibliographic notes. The basis of the (significantly less accurate) film The Imitation Game.

Author site → BiographyWWII
Biography
The Woman Who Smashed Codes
Jason Fagone
2017 · ISBN 978-0062430489

Reconstruction of Elizebeth Smith Friedman's career from Riverbank Laboratories through the Coast Guard and WWII Abwehr work, drawing on her papers at the Marshall Library (opened 2008) and Coast Guard records. The definitive account of the FBI's deliberate erasure of her contribution and the restoration of the historical record. Essential companion to the Elizebeth Friedman exhibit.

Author site → BiographyWomen in Crypto
Memoir
Between Silk and Cyanide
Leo Marks
1998 · ISBN 978-0684864228

Leo Marks’s memoir of code work for Britain’s Special Operations Executive. It discusses poem codes, agents’ communications and the pressures of wartime key handling. As a retrospective personal account, it should be compared with archival and scholarly sources. Companion to the Leo Marks exhibit.

Popular History
Seizing the Enigma
David Kahn
1991 · revised 2012 · ISBN 978-1591146650

Kahn's focused account of Allied capture operations against German naval Enigma material — pinches from weather ships, trawlers, and submarines. Explains why physical capture of key material was as important as mathematical cryptanalysis and how the two combined in the Battle of the Atlantic. A shorter, more readable Kahn than The Codebreakers.

HistoryNavalWWII
Popular History
Battle of Wits
Stephen Budiansky
2000 · ISBN 978-0684859323

The best single-volume Allied codebreaking history of WWII — Bletchley Park, Arlington Hall, OP-20-G, the Pacific war, and the cooperation between them. Budiansky integrates the Polish, British, and American contributions to Enigma in a way Hinsley's official history could not, and treats Friedman's Purple solution and JN-25 work with equal weight. Highly readable; rigorously sourced.

HistoryWWII
Modern Intelligence History
GCHQ: The Uncensored Story of Britain's Most Secret Intelligence Agency
Richard J. Aldrich
2010 · ISBN 978-0007278473

A history of British signals intelligence, including wartime codebreaking, the Cold War and intelligence alliances. The 2010 edition predates the 2013 Snowden disclosures; do not treat it as an account of that later episode or as the authorized GCHQ history.

Primary Source Archives

  • NSA Center for Cryptologic History
    Fort Meade, Maryland

    The NSA's public history arm publishes declassified monographs, oral history transcripts, and the back issues of the internal Cryptolog journal. The VENONA documents (2,900 decrypted messages), the Friedman Documents collection, and the technical histories of SIGABA, SIGSALY, and early NSA computing are free downloads. The single richest primary-source archive for 20th-century American cryptologic history.

    nsa.gov/history → Primary SourcesFree
  • Bletchley Park Trust Archive
    Milton Keynes, UK

    Bletchley Park preserves records, objects and accounts of the wartime codebreaking operation. Its Roll of Honour is a research resource, not a complete census. The working Colossus and Bombe reconstructions are at the separately operated National Museum of Computing on the Bletchley Park estate; check each institution’s visitor information.

    bletchleypark.org.uk → Primary SourcesWWII
  • UK National Archives — HW Department
    Kew, London

    The National Archives uses the department reference HW for records of GCHQ and its predecessor, the Government Code and Cypher School. It contains multiple series, including signals intelligence and technical histories. The official research guide explains how to search them and check viewing or download arrangements.

    GCHQ and GCCS records guide → Primary SourcesWWII
  • US National Archives — Record Group 457
    College Park, Maryland

    RG 457 is the primary NSA / ASA / AFSA record group — the largest collection of US SIGINT materials available to researchers. Contains VENONA working papers, original MAGIC (“Purple”) intercept files, raw WWII intercept traffic, and NSA's own historical studies of major programs. Many materials require FOIA requests; others are open access. The definitive first stop for primary-source American SIGINT research.

    RG 457 finding aid → Primary SourcesSIGINT
  • Friedman Collection — George C. Marshall Foundation
    Virginia Military Institute, Lexington, VA

    The foundation’s collection descriptions identify separate William F. Friedman and Elizebeth S. Friedman holdings. Consult their finding aids for the actual scope and access conditions. Other Friedman papers are held by additional archives; a general collection link does not establish that every named document is digitized.

    Collection descriptions — see Elizebeth and William Friedman → Primary SourcesBiography
  • Voynich Manuscript — Beinecke MS 408
    Yale University Library

    Full high-resolution scans of every page of the Voynich Manuscript are freely available through the Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library. The standard research corpus. Researchers should also consult René Zandbergen's voynich.nu, the most thorough public source on provenance, hand identification, and transcript history — in more depth than any single publication.

    Beinecke digital scans → Primary SourcesUnsolvedFree
  • Crypto Museum
    Paul Reuvers & Marc Simons · Eindhoven, Netherlands · cryptomuseum.com

    The most comprehensive online archive of cipher machines, spy radios, burst encoders, and intelligence-related communications equipment. Detailed technical reconstructions, photographs, and operator documentation for hundreds of cipher machines from the early 1900s through the Cold War. The Cipher Museum's Hagelin family sidebar (M-209 exhibit) and SIGSALY exhibit cite Crypto Museum Foundation documentation directly. Cipher Museum is listed in Crypto Museum's Virtual Museums directory (April 2026).

    cryptomuseum.com → Peer InstitutionHardwareFree

Reference · Federal

The authoritative source for current US cryptographic standards: AES (FIPS 197), SHA-2 / SHA-3 (FIPS 180-4 / 202), the post-quantum standards ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA (FIPS 203/204/205, finalized 2024), and the SP 800 guidance series. Books Behind the New Experiments

I. J. Good, Donald Michie and Geoffrey Timms, General Report on Tunny (1945), §§23A–C, printed pp. 78–80 (collected PDF pp. 81–83), supports the known-pattern setting method in the Colossus counting experiment. Its synthetic traffic and modern null simulations are identified separately.

George Lasry, A Methodology for the Cryptanalysis of Classical Ciphers with Search Metaheuristics (2018), §§4.5–4.7 and §§5.3.2–5.3.4, printed pp. 57–60 and 69–78 (PDF pp. 78–81 and 90–99), supports evaluation discipline and the bounded columnar search. The museum's adaptations and measured limits are stated on the exhibit.
